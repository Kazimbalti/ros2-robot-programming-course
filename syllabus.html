🎯 Course Description

This is a hands-on robotics and ROS 2 course for students meeting ROS for the first time. After Mission Zero (Linux survival training) and Lecture 1 (installation), it runs in two parts. Every lecture follows the same learning format: outcomes → warm-up → the idea in 10 minutes → step-by-step labs with expected output → your-turn activities → common errors → exit ticket → homework.

🧩 How the course is built

Part A: ROS 2 Bootcamp (Lectures 2–7) follows the ROS2 Boot Camp chapters of the course book (pp. 9–104): nodes, topics, services, custom interfaces, parameters, launch files, ROS 2 bags and the "Catch Them All" project, all practised on turtlesim.

Part B: ROS 2 on the LIMO (Lectures 8–14) applies the same skills to the AgileX LIMO: driving with code and odometry, URDF/TF/RViz, LiDAR and IMU, camera and computer vision, SLAM and AMCL, Nav2 and actions, then the physical robot and the final "LIMO Delivery" mission.

Projects: Build Your Own Robot I–III (after Lecture 14, optional) apply everything to a robot the students build themselves: motors and power, a ROS 2 driver, URDF, sensors, a Gazebo twin and a 3D-printed cover.

📝 Course Policies

Attendance

Lecture attendance is expected; laboratory attendance is mandatory because of shared robots, hardware checkouts, and safety procedures. Notify the instructor before missing a lab to arrange a make-up. Full policy on the Attendance page.

Late Work

Each student has a budget of four late days per term, applied in whole-day units, maximum 2 per assignment. After the budget is exhausted, submissions lose 15% per day for up to three days, then receive zero credit. Exams, the capstone demo, and final presentations cannot use late days.

Collaboration & Academic Integrity

Discussing concepts, error messages, and approaches is encouraged. The code and written answers you submit must be your own. Copying code between individuals or teams, sharing solutions, or submitting generated text as your own analysis are integrity violations and are reported per institutional policy. Cite any external code, tutorial, or dataset you build on.

AI Tool Use

AI assistants are permitted for debugging, boilerplate, and explanation. They are not permitted for generating complete lab reports, full driver nodes submitted as your work, or exam answers. Every submission must include a one-line note stating where and how AI tools were used.

Safety

Lecture 14 and the final mission use the physical LIMO: rechargeable batteries, spinning wheels and a Class 1 LiDAR. Follow the six safety rules in Lecture 14: robots on the floor only, one safety officer per team, low speeds until code is proven, a stop command always ready, a clear arena, and never charge batteries unattended. Report any damaged battery or frayed wiring immediately.

Accommodations & Well-being

Students with approved accommodations should share documentation within the first two weeks so arrangements can be made. If anything — illness, workload, or circumstances — is getting in the way, contact the instructor early; timely support is far easier to arrange than retroactive relief.
