🎯 Sample Questions

Representative — not exhaustive
  1. Wheel odometry says the robot has driven straight for 5 m; the LiDAR scan-match disagrees by 12 cm and 3°. Explain what an EKF does with these two inputs and why it does not simply average them.
  2. In a Nav2 run the global plan is fine but the robot oscillates near obstacles. Name the component most likely responsible and two parameters you would change.
  3. Derive the inverse kinematics for a differential-drive robot: given v and ω, wheel radius r, track width L, give the left/right wheel angular velocities.
  4. Your DIY robot drives well in Gazebo but veers left on the bench. Give three physical causes and the ROS-side calibration or fix for each.
  5. Compare SLAM and AMCL: what does each assume about the map, and when do you run which?

✅ How to Prepare

  • Rebuild the Lecture 10 mission from scratch in sim; be able to explain every node in the graph.
  • Redo the Lecture 9 drift/fusion plots and be able to read them aloud.
  • Write the differential-drive kinematics from memory, both directions.
  • For each LIMO subsystem, write one sentence on the ROS 2 interface and one realistic failure mode.
  • Rehearse the capstone defence out loud against a timer; anticipate "why did you choose…" for every major decision.
  • Review the glossary and "Common Mistakes" boxes across Lectures 8–14.
🔗 Capstone & the final are graded together

The oral defence (Part D) is scored with the capstone rubric. The written capstone report is due at the start of the final exam slot and cannot use late days.
