🤖 Introduction

Welcome to the fantastic world of ROBOTICS!

First, you must understand what a robot is.

A robot is a programmable machine designed to carry out tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously, often resembling or imitating human or animal actions.!

Robots are equipped with various sensors, actuators, and mechanisms that enable them to perceive their environment, make decisions, and execute actions accordingly. The concept of a robot encompasses a wide range of devices, from simple industrial arms to sophisticated humanoid robots and autonomous vehicles.

Robots can be of many shapes and sizes for infinite applications and complexity. However, there are specific components that any robot MUST HAVE.

To understand it better, you will compare a robot to a human and see the components shared in a robotic form.

🧠 ROS2 Robot Architecture

This is a complete, ready-to-teach robotics and ROS 2 curriculum built specifically around the AgileX LIMO — a four-in-one mobile robot that can switch between differential, Ackermann, Mecanum, and tracked drive bases on the same chassis. It is organised in two lecture parts plus a projects part:

🧩 How the course is built

Part A: ROS 2 Bootcamp (Lectures 2–7) covers the ROS 2 core, step by step: nodes, topics, services, custom interfaces, parameters, launch files, bags and the "Catch Them All" turtlesim project. Every idea is practised on turtlesim first, where nothing can break.

Part B: ROS 2 on the LIMO (Lectures 8–14) applies the same skills to the AgileX LIMO: driving with code, odometry, URDF/TF/RViz, LiDAR and IMU, camera and vision, SLAM and AMCL, Nav2 and actions, and finally the real robot and a final mission.

Projects: Build Your Own Robot I–III come after Lecture 14: build "Forge", your own ROS 2 robot, from motors and power to a driver node, URDF, sensors, a Gazebo twin and a 3D-printed cover.

Every lecture uses the same learning format: learning outcomes → warm-up → the idea in 10 minutes → step-by-step labs with expected output → your-turn activities with hints and solutions → common errors → exit ticket → homework. About 80% of each session is hands-on.

Each lecture is a 3-hour session with interactive simulators, games and knowledge checks built into the page. Lectures build on each other in order, and the code from Part A (especially the go-to-goal controller from Lecture 7) is reused on the LIMO in Part B. The older, longer notes are kept at the bottom of the list as optional extended reference notes for students who want more depth.

👤 Who This Course Is For

Students, hobbyists, or engineers with basic Python and command-line comfort, working through a full university-style or bootcamp-style module on mobile robotics. No prior ROS or Linux experience is assumed — Lecture 1 starts from a blank Windows/Mac machine.

🌍 Turtle first, then simulation, then the real robot

Part A teaches every ROS 2 concept on turtlesim, where mistakes are free. Part B moves the same code to the LIMO in Gazebo, so a classroom never has to wait for robot time. Lecture 14 then runs your own nodes on the physical LIMO and names the sim-to-real differences (noise, slip, lighting, timing) that you measure yourself.
